What articles do

English has three articles: a, an, and the. An article comes before a noun. It tells your reader whether you mean one specific thing or any one of many.

  • I read an article about sleep. The reader does not know which article. It is one of many.
  • I read the article about sleep. The reader knows which article. Maybe the instructor assigned it.

Many languages have no articles, so this part of English takes practice. A missing article rarely stops a reader from understanding you. Still, articles appear in almost every sentence, so a small improvement shows everywhere in your writing.

Key terms
Noun:
a word for a person, place, thing, or idea.
Countable noun:
a noun you can count. It has a plural form: one essay, two essays.
Uncountable noun:
a noun you cannot count. It has no plural form: research, information.

Three questions for every noun

When you are not sure, ask these questions in order. Stop at the first “yes.”

  1. Does my reader know exactly which one I mean? If yes, use the.
  2. Is the noun singular and countable? If yes, use a or an.
  3. Is the noun plural or uncountable, and am I speaking in general? If yes, use no article.

When does my reader know which one?

  • You already mentioned it. I wrote a draft. The draft was too short.
  • Your sentence identifies it. the article that we read this week, the first paragraph
  • There is only one. the author of an article, the instructor of your class, the sun

A or an?

Listen to the first sound of the next word. The spelling does not decide.

  • Use a before a consonant sound: a draft, a university, a one-page summary.
  • Use an before a vowel sound: an essay, an hour, an MLA citation.

Three common problems

Watch for these

1. Uncountable nouns in college writing

These nouns are common in college, and all of them are uncountable in English: research, evidence, information, advice, feedback, homework, equipment, software. Do not use a or an with them, and do not add -s.

Uncountable nouns
Not thisWrite this
I did a research.I did research. / I read a study.
The essay needs more evidences.The essay needs more evidence.
She gave me an advice.She gave me some advice. / She gave me a piece of advice.
I found many informations.I found a lot of information.
I finished my homeworks.I finished my homework. / I finished my assignments.

2. A singular countable noun with nothing before it

A singular countable noun cannot stand alone. It needs an article or another small word such as my, this, or one. Use only one of them.

Singular countable nouns
Not thisWrite this
I wrote essay about my job.I wrote an essay about my job.
The my essay is finished.My essay is finished.

3. The in general statements

When you speak about a whole group in general, use a plural noun with no article.

  • General: Students need feedback. This means students everywhere.
  • Specific: The students in my group need feedback. This means one group that the reader can identify.

Phrases you will use often

You can learn these phrases as whole pieces. The article is already correct.

  • To write about a readingAccording to the author, .The main idea of the article is .In the first paragraph, .
  • To give supportThis is an example of .A study by shows that .
  • To explain your own writingThe purpose of this essay is to .As a result, .

Practice

Choose a, an, the, or no article for each blank.

  1. I wrote essay about my first job.
  2. essay that I wrote last week needs more support.
  3. My instructor gave me feedback on my draft.
  4. She said that I need stronger thesis.
  5. I need to find information about wages.
  6. It took me hour to revise one paragraph.
  7. students often work while they attend college.

Then correct this sentence: “I did a research for my homeworks.”

Show suggested answers
  1. an First mention, singular and countable. Essay begins with a vowel sound.
  2. The The words that I wrote last week identify which essay.
  3. No article Feedback is uncountable. Some feedback is also correct.
  4. a Singular and countable, and the reader does not know which thesis yet. Stronger begins with a consonant sound.
  5. No article Information is uncountable. Some information is also correct.
  6. an The h in hour is silent, so the word begins with a vowel sound.
  7. No article The sentence is about students in general. The first word is Students, with a capital letter.

Corrected sentence: I did research for my homework. Both nouns are uncountable, so they take no a and no -s.
