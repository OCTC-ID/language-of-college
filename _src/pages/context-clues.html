What context clues are

College readings have many new words. You do not need to look up every one. Often, the words around a new word, called the context, give you enough information to understand it. These hints are called context clues.

Using context clues saves time, and it keeps you reading. Stopping for every word makes it hard to follow the main idea.

When to stop and look a word up

  • The word appears many times.
  • The word is in the title, a heading, or the assignment directions.
  • You cannot understand the sentence without it, even after you use the clues.

Otherwise, make a guess, mark the word, and keep reading. You can check your guess later.

Key terms
Context:
the words and sentences around a word.
Clue:
a piece of information that helps you find an answer.
Prefix:
a word part at the beginning of a word, such as re- in rewrite.
Suffix:
a word part at the end of a word, such as -tion in information.

Four kinds of clues

Context clues and their signal words
Kind of clueSignal wordsExample
Definition: the writer explains the wordis, means, is called, refers to, commas, parenthesesA stipend, or a small fixed payment, helps students pay for books.
Example: the writer gives examplessuch as, for example, includingMany perishable foods, such as milk, fish, and fresh fruit, must stay cold.
Contrast: the writer gives an oppositebut, however, unlike, instead ofUnlike her reticent brother, Maria talks to everyone.
Word parts: the word itselfa prefix, a root, or a suffix you knowThe plan was unworkable. (un- = not, -able = can be: it could not work.)

Language focus: word parts

Language focus

Prefixes and suffixes in college words

Many academic words are built from parts. If you know a part, you can often guess a new word. A prefix often changes the meaning. A suffix often changes the kind of word: a noun, a verb, or an adjective.

Common word parts
PartMeaningExamples
un-, in-, im-, dis-notunclear, incomplete, impossible, disagree
re-againrevise, rewrite, reconsider
pre-beforeprerequisite, preview
-tion, -ment, -nessmakes a nouninformation, assignment, awareness
-ize, -ifymakes a verbsummarize, clarify
-able, -ive, -almakes an adjectivereliable, effective, personal

Word families

One root can make several words. Knowing the family helps you read and write: analyze (verb), analysis (noun), analytical (adjective).

Watch for this

Word parts give you a guess, not a promise. In- means “not” in incomplete, but it means “in” in include. Check the guess against the sentence.

Notes you can write

When you guess a word, write a short note in the margin or in your notebook. Fill in each blank with your own words.

  • Your guessI think means .
  • Your reasonThe clue is .The word part means .
  • To check laterLook up . It appears times.

Practice

Guess the meaning of each bold word. Name the kind of clue.

  1. The nurse checked the patient’s vital signs, including heart rate, breathing, and temperature.
  2. The first draft was verbose, but the final draft was short and clear.
  3. The company plans to relocate its office to a larger building.
  4. Erosion, the slow loss of soil to wind and water, is a problem on many farms.
Show suggested answers
  1. Basic body measurements that show health. An example clue: including introduces heart rate, breathing, and temperature.
  2. Using too many words. A contrast clue: but introduces the opposite, short and clear.
  3. To move to a new place. A word-part clue: re- (again) + locate (place). The sentence also says where: a larger building.
  4. The slow loss of soil. A definition clue: the commas surround the meaning.

Next step

Try this with your next reading

  1. Read one page without stopping to look up words.
  2. Mark each new word, and write your guess next to it.
  3. After the page, look up only the words that appear often or that you still need.
  4. Check your guesses. How many were close?
