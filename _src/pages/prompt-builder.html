Check a prompt with Copilot

      
If you already have a prompt, Microsoft Copilot can read it the way a careful student would and show you what is missing. Copilot is part of the college’s Microsoft 365 package. Copy the text below into Copilot, then paste your own prompt at the end.

      

        
You are helping a college instructor revise an assignment prompt so that every student can understand it, including students who read English as an additional language. Do not change what the assignment asks students to do. Do not make the assignment easier.

Step 1. List what a student could not tell from this prompt. Check each of these:
- the task and its main verb
- the topic and its limits
- the intended reader
- each requirement, and its number
- what is optional
- how the work is graded, including how much grammar counts
- the AI level (No AI, AI-supported, or AI-integrated)
- if the level is AI-supported, exactly which uses are allowed and which are not
- if the level is AI-integrated, the tool, the steps for using AI, what students must do themselves, and what they turn in to show how they used AI
- which language tools are allowed (dictionary, translation tool, grammar checker)
- where to submit the work and where to find the due date

Step 2. List every idiom, cultural reference, and undefined academic term in the prompt. Suggest plain wording for each one.

Step 3. List any requirement that is worded as a suggestion, such as "you might want to."

Step 4. Rewrite the prompt under these headings: Purpose, Task, Reader, Requirements, Optional, Grading, Tools. Use short sentences and common words. Use "must" for requirements. Where information is missing, write [INSTRUCTOR: add ...]. Do not invent requirements, numbers, or policies.

Or start from scratch with Copilot

      
If you do not have a prompt yet and prefer a conversation to a form, use this version. Copilot asks you one question at a time and writes the prompt when you say “ready.” Allow five to ten minutes.

      

        
You are helping a college instructor write a clear assignment prompt that every student can understand, including students who read English as an additional language.

Interview me one question at a time. Wait for my answer before you ask the next question. Do not draft anything until I say "ready."

Ask about each of these, in this order:
1. the skill this assignment builds, and where students will use it again
2. the type of text and the task, named with one verb
3. what that verb means in my course
4. the topic and its limits
5. the intended reader
6. each requirement, with a number (length in words, number of sources, format)
7. what is optional
8. what counts most in the grade, and how much grammar and word choice count
9. the AI level (No AI, AI-supported, or AI-integrated). If I answer AI-supported, ask which uses are allowed (for example, brainstorming or feedback on a draft) and which are not. If I answer AI-integrated, ask which tool students use, what they do with AI, what they must do themselves, and what they turn in to show how they used AI
10. which language tools are allowed (dictionary, translation tool, grammar checker)
11. where students submit the work and find the due date

If my answer is vague, ask one follow-up question. If I say "skip," move on.

When I say "ready," write the prompt under these headings: Purpose, Task, Reader, Requirements, Optional, Grading, Tools, Submitting. Use short sentences and common words. Do not use idioms. Use "must" for requirements. For anything I skipped, write [INSTRUCTOR: add ...]. Do not invent requirements, numbers, or college policies.

Start with question 1.
