What a clear sentence does

A clear sentence lets the reader understand it the first time. In college writing, clear is better than complicated. Instructors do not give more points for long words or long sentences. They give points for ideas the reader can follow.

This page gives five ways to make a sentence clearer. Use them when you revise, not while you write your first draft.

Key terms
Specific:
exact. Three weeks is more specific than a while.
Vague:
not exact or not clear. Things and stuff are vague words.
Wordy:
using more words than the idea needs.
Pronoun:
a short word that stands for a noun, such as it, this, or they.

Five ways to make a sentence clearer

Five ways to revise a sentence
WayUnclearClearer
1. Use specific wordsThe class had a lot of good things.The class had short videos and weekly quizzes.
2. Say who does the actionMistakes were made in the report.Our team made two mistakes in the report.
3. Cut extra wordsDue to the fact that I was sick, I was not able to attend.Because I was sick, I could not attend.
4. Put one main idea in each sentenceI like nursing and my clinical is on Fridays and the hospital is far.I like nursing. My clinical is on Fridays, at a hospital far from home.
5. Make this and it clearTuition went up, and the bus schedule changed. This made it hard to attend.Tuition went up, and the bus schedule changed. These changes made it hard to attend.

Language focus: this + a noun

Language focus

Point back clearly

Writers often begin a sentence with This or It to point back to the sentence before. When the sentence before has more than one idea, the reader cannot tell which one you mean. Add a noun after this to name it.

This alone and this + a noun
UnclearClear
This shows that students need support.This survey shows that students need support.
This is a serious problem.This delay is a serious problem.
It made the results less reliable.The small sample made the results less reliable.
Watch for this

Use this with a singular noun and these with a plural noun: this change, these changes.

Practice

Make each sentence clearer. Use the way named in brackets.

  1. The reading had a lot of interesting stuff. [specific words]
  2. The essay was not finished on time. [say who]
  3. In order to pass the class, it is important to attend. [cut extra words]
  4. The clinic closed and the bus stopped. This hurt the town. [this + a noun]
Show suggested answers
  1. One possible answer: The reading had three surprising facts about sleep. Replace a lot of interesting stuff with exactly what the reading had.
  2. One possible answer: I did not finish the essay on time. The new sentence names the person.
  3. One possible answer: To pass the class, attend every week. In order to becomes to, and it is important to becomes a direct statement.
  4. One possible answer: These two losses hurt the town. There are two ideas before this, so these + a plural noun names both.

Revise three sentences in your draft

  2. Find a sentence that begins with This or It. Is it clear what it points to?
  3. Find your longest sentence. Can it be two sentences?
