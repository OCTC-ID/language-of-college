Two things to check before you use a tool

You probably use tools for language every day: a translation app, spell check, a dictionary, or an AI chat tool such as Microsoft Copilot. In college, the rules for these tools can change from one assignment to the next. Before you use a tool on an assignment, check two things.

1. The AI use label

At OCTC, each assignment has an AI use label at the top of its directions. Your instructor’s AI Course Policy in the syllabus has the rules for the whole course. There are three labels:

The three AI use labels
LabelIn shortWhat it means
No AIPractice on your own.You do the work without AI. The skill itself is what you are practicing.
AI-supportedYou lead. AI supports.You may use AI for the parts your instructor names. The thinking and the final work are yours.
AI-integratedUsing AI is part of the task.Using AI well is part of the assignment. Your instructor tells you how.

For more about the labels and the 3 Qs to ask each time you use AI, see AI Literacy: Start Where You Are (opens in a new tab).

2. The rules for language tools

The label is about AI. It does not always tell you about a dictionary, spell check, or a translation tool. Look for a separate line in the directions, often called Tools. If you cannot find one, ask your instructor.

Key terms
AI use label:
the line at the top of an assignment that says how you may use AI on it.
Translation tool:
an app or website that changes text from one language to another.
Grammar checker:
a tool that marks possible errors in grammar, spelling, or punctuation.
Rewrite:
to write a sentence again in different words. Many tools now offer to rewrite your sentences for you.

One question for any tool: who leads?

Some tools give you information, and you decide what to write. Other tools write for you. Ask yourself: Am I leading, or is the tool leading?

Language tools and who leads
What you doWho leads?What to check
Look up a word in a dictionaryYouThe Tools line. A few assignments, such as some vocabulary quizzes, do not allow it.
Use spell check to find errors, then fix them yourselfYouThe Tools line.
Translate part of a reading to understand itYouThe Tools line. Many instructors allow this, but ask if the directions do not say.
Listen to a reading with text-to-speechYouThe Tools line. If you use it as an approved accommodation, follow your accommodation.
Translate your own writing into EnglishThe toolThe AI use label and the Tools line. The tool writes the English sentences.
Click “rewrite” or accept a suggested new sentenceThe toolThe AI use label. A tool that rewrites your sentences counts as AI, even if it calls itself a grammar or translation tool.
Ask Copilot to explain a grammar ruleYouThe AI use label. On a No AI assignment, ask a tutor instead.
Why it matters

When a tool rewrites your sentences, it can change your meaning, and the words are no longer yours. Your instructor wants to read your ideas in your words. Small errors in your own sentences are part of learning.

Example: assignment directions with a label and a Tools line

Here is the top of one set of assignment directions. Each number matches a note below.

Language focus: saying exactly what you want to use a tool for

Language focus

to + verb, or for + noun

A specific question gets a specific answer. When you ask about a tool, say what you want to use it for. English has two common patterns for this.

Two ways to say the purpose
PatternExample
use [tool] to + base verbMay I use a translation tool to understand the article?
use [tool] for + nounMay I use Copilot for brainstorming?
use [tool] for + nounIs a dictionary allowed for the vocabulary quiz?
Watch for this

Do not put for and to together. Write I used a dictionary to check the meaning. Do not write “I used a dictionary for to check the meaning.”

Compare a general question and a specific one:

  • General: Can I use AI?
  • Specific: May I use Copilot to explain the grammar comments on my draft if I make the changes myself?

Practice

  1. Who leads: you or the tool?
    • A. You paste your paragraph into a translation app and copy the English version.
    • B. You look up the word “hypothesis” in a dictionary.
    • C. A grammar tool offers a new version of your sentence, and you click “Accept.”
  2. An assignment says “AI use: No AI.” It has no Tools line. You want to use a dictionary. What should you do?
  3. Make this question more specific: “Can I use translation?”
  4. Correct this sentence: “I used Copilot for to explain the rule.”
Show suggested answers
  1. A: the tool. B: you. C: the tool. In A and C, the tool writes the sentences. In B, the dictionary gives you information, and you decide what to write.
  2. Ask your instructor. The label is about AI. It does not say whether a dictionary is allowed. A short question before you start is easy to answer.
  3. One possible answer: “May I use a translation tool to understand the second reading?” It names the tool, the purpose, and the part of the work. Other answers are possible.
  4. I used Copilot to explain the rule. Use to + base verb, or for + noun, but not both: I used Copilot for an explanation of the rule.

Next step

Before you use any tool on an assignment

  1. Find the AI use label at the top of the directions.
  2. Find the Tools line, if there is one.
  3. Ask yourself: am I leading, or is the tool leading?
  4. If the directions do not answer your question, ask your instructor before you start.
  5. If you use a tool in a significant way, write it down in your writing record.
