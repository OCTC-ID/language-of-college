Instructor Companion

Practical guidance for faculty and staff who teach students with a wide range of language and school backgrounds. Every page includes examples and wording you can copy, and most pair with a student page you can link from Blackboard.

Pages in the Instructor Companion

  • Writing a clear assignment promptSix practices for writing assignment prompts that state expectations openly, with a before-and-after example and wording to copy.
  • Setting expectations for discussions and journalsEight practices for stating the unwritten rules of discussions and journals, with a before-and-after example and wording to copy.
  • Tool: Assignment prompt builderA fill-in tool that assembles a clear assignment prompt, plus a Copilot prompt for checking the clarity of an existing assignment.
  • Rubrics and where language accuracy fitsHow to decide what language accuracy is worth in a rubric, six practices, a before-and-after language row, and wording to copy.

  • Setting expectations for translation, grammar tools, and AISeven practices for stating which language tools and AI uses an assignment allows, with a Tools line, a use note, and syllabus wording to copy.
  • Talking about writing process and authorshipSeven practices for process expectations and authorship conversations that do not rely on AI detection or treat language differences as evidence.
  • Knowing your students' language backgroundsWho multilingual learners are, six practices for learning about students without singling anyone out, and an optional first-week question to copy.
  • Terms for language learners: what to use, and whenA glossary of terms for language learners, from ESL and ELL to multilingual writer and Generation 1.5, with practices for choosing respectful, accurate terms.

Linking a page from Blackboard

  1. Open the page you want students to use, and copy the address from the browser.
  2. Add the link where students need it: in the assignment directions, a module, or an announcement. A link right before a task gets more use than a list at the start of the term.
  3. Say why, in one sentence. Each instructor page has a “Link for students” section with wording you can copy.
The addresses stay the same

Pages are updated in place and are never renamed or moved, so a link you add this term will still work next term. Due dates, points, and course rules stay in Blackboard. The one page that changes each term, This semester, keeps the same address.

How the toolkit is written

  • Student pages use no labels. They describe skills and tasks, so any student can use them without identifying as a language learner. Instructor pages use “multilingual learners.” See Terms for language learners.
  • Student pages are written for an intermediate English reader: short sentences, no idioms, and English examples that stay in English when a browser translates the page.
  • AI guidance follows OCTC’s AI use labels (No AI, AI-supported, AI-integrated) from the AI Literacy Faculty Playbook (opens in a new tab). Translation, dictionaries, and grammar checkers are addressed separately, in a Tools line.
  • AI detection is never treated as proof, and language differences are never treated as evidence of AI use.
  • Sources are named on each page. Practices without a verified source are labeled as practitioner advice from OCTC faculty development work.
  • Offline copies: students without steady internet access can download the whole toolkit as a PDF or EPUB from the home page.
