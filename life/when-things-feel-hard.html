College Life

When Things Feel Hard

Use this page when you are stressed, worried, or having a hard week, and you want to know where to get help.

About 7 minutes.

If you need help right now

If you are in danger, call 911. If you are thinking about suicide or are in emotional crisis, call or text 988, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org (opens in a new tab). The 988 Lifeline is available 24 hours a day, every day. Text and chat are also available in Spanish, and interpretation is available.

What many students notice

College adds new pressure to a life that is often already full. Deadlines, tests, money, work, family, health, and adjusting to a new place can all arrive at the same time. Feeling stressed, tired, or worried is common, and it does not mean you are failing.

  • Hard weeks happen to everyone. Many students have at least one during a term.
  • Problems are easier to solve early. A small problem can grow if nobody knows about it.
  • Asking for help is a skill. Students who use support services are not weaker. They are using what the college offers.

What helps

  1. Name the problem. Write one sentence: what is hard right now? A problem with a name is easier to solve.
  2. Choose one next step. Not the whole solution. One step, such as one email or one phone call.
  3. Tell your instructor early. You do not need to share details. See the example below.
  4. Talk to someone. A friend, a family member, an advisor, an instructor you trust, or a counselor.
  5. Take care of the basics. Sleep, food, and a short break each day make every other problem easier to handle.

Language focus: saying enough, not everything

Language focus

General words for private situations

In a U.S. college, you can ask for help without explaining private details. These general phrases are polite and complete. Many instructors and staff will not ask for more.

General phrases
Instead of explainingYou can say
details about a family memberI am dealing with a family situation.
details about an illnessI have a health issue this week.
details about money or housingI am dealing with a personal situation.
a long storyThings are difficult right now.
Watch for this

Use dealing with + a noun: I am dealing with a family situation. Do not write “I am dealing a family situation.”

Some situations, such as a disability, may need documentation for an official accommodation. The Counseling Center can explain that process.

Sentence starters

Fill in each blank with your own words.

  • To an instructorI am dealing with this week. Could I ?
  • To a counselor or advisorI am having a hard time with .I am not sure where to start. Can you help me with ?
  • To a friend or classmateThis week is hard for me. Could you ?

Where to get help at OCTC

Counseling Center

OCTC counselors help with academic concerns and with personal concerns that affect your progress, such as stress, test anxiety, money, or transportation. Personal counseling is short-term, and counselors can refer you to services in the community for longer support. The Counseling Center also works with students with disabilities on classroom accommodations.

  • Where: Campus Center, Upper Level, Main Campus
  • Phone: 270-686-4418
  • OCTC Counseling Center (opens in a new tab)

Basic Needs Hub

Help with food, housing, utilities, emergencies, transportation, health care, and money. OCTC says, “You do not need to be in a crisis to use these services.”

  • OCTC Basic Needs Hub (opens in a new tab)

More support

  • OCTC Student Support Services (opens in a new tab), including childcare resources, TRIO, and veterans services.
  • OCTC Student Support guide (opens in a new tab), which lists tutoring, advising, accessibility, counseling, and everyday needs on one page.

Check each service’s page for current hours and how to make an appointment. If you study online, ask about options that work for you.

Next step

If this week is hard

  1. Write one sentence about what is hard right now.
  2. Choose one next step, and do it today.
  3. If it affects a class, tell your instructor before the due date.
  4. If it is bigger than one step, contact the Counseling Center.
