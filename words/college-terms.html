College words

College Terms You Will Hear in Your First Weeks

Use this page when you see a word in your syllabus, in Blackboard, or in class that you do not know.

About 10 minutes. Includes practice with answers.

Why these words matter

In your first weeks, instructors and staff use many words that are special to college. They may not explain them, because they use them every day. Knowing these words helps you read your syllabus, follow announcements, and ask clear questions.

This page groups common terms by where you will meet them. You do not need to learn them all today. Come back to the page when you see a word you do not know.

Rules can be different in each course

This page gives general meanings. The exact rules, such as how late work is graded or how to drop a class, are in your syllabus or come from your advisor. When a rule matters, check there.

Words about your courses

Course words
WordMeaning
syllabusThe document that explains a course: its schedule, rules, grading, and how to contact the instructor. Read it in the first week.
semester, termThe period of time when a course meets, such as Fall or Spring.
sectionOne group of students in a course. The same course can have several sections, at different times or online.
credit hourThe unit that measures a course. Most courses are three credit hours. Your degree requires a certain number of them.
full-time, part-timeHow many credit hours you take in a semester. The number for full-time can matter for financial aid. Ask your advisor.
prerequisiteA course you must finish before you can take another course.
corequisiteA course you must take at the same time as another course.
drop, withdrawTo leave a course. When you leave affects your grade and your money. Talk to your advisor first.
advisorA person who helps you choose courses and plan your degree.
BlackboardThe website where your courses live: assignments, grades, announcements, and messages.

Words about assignments and grades

Assignment and grade words
WordMeaning
assignmentA piece of work your instructor asks you to do, such as an essay, a quiz, or a discussion post.
prompt, directionsThe instructions for an assignment. See Understanding a writing prompt.
due dateThe day and time when an assignment must be turned in.
late workWork turned in after the due date. Each instructor has a rule for it. Find it in the syllabus.
rubricA chart that shows how an assignment will be graded. See Reading a rubric.
participationTaking an active part in class. It is often part of the grade.
attendanceBeing present in class. In an online class, it can mean logging in and doing the work each week.
GPAGrade point average. One number that shows your average grade in all your courses.
academic integrityDoing your own work honestly, and giving credit when you use other people’s words or ideas.
plagiarismUsing someone else’s words or ideas without giving them credit. Colleges treat it as a serious problem.

Words about places and help

Places and help
WordMeaning
office hoursTimes when an instructor is available to meet with students. Some instructors call them student hours. Every student is welcome.
Teaching and Learning Center (TLC)OCTC’s free tutoring and writing help center. See This semester for hours.
tutorA person who helps you with coursework outside of class. See Working with a tutor.
libraryA place for books, articles, and research help, in person and online. OCTC Library (opens in a new tab)
library databaseAn online collection of articles and books that the library pays for. You use it to find reliable sources.

Language focus: verb phrases you will hear in college

Language focus

Two-word verbs

English often uses a verb + a small word such as in, up, or for to make a new meaning. These are called phrasal verbs. The meaning of the pair can be different from the meaning of each word.

Phrasal verbs in college
PhraseMeaningExample
turn in, hand insubmitTurn in your essay in Blackboard.
sign up forregister forI signed up for a tutoring session.
make updo missed work laterCan I make up the quiz?
fall behindnot keep up with the scheduleI fell behind in the second week.
catch upreach the schedule againI need to catch up on the reading.
look overread quickly to checkPlease look over my outline.
drop outleave school completelyShe did not drop out; she dropped one class.
Watch for this

Drop a class means leaving one course. Drop out means leaving college. The small word out changes the meaning.

Sentence starters

Use these when you meet a word you do not know. Fill in each blank with your own words.

  • To ask about a wordWhat does mean in the syllabus?Could you explain what means for this course?
  • To ask about a ruleWhat is the rule for in this class?If I , what should I do?

Practice

  1. A course lists “Prerequisite: MAT 110.” You have not taken MAT 110. Can you take the course now?
  2. Where do you find your instructor’s rule for late work?
  3. Choose the correct phrase: “I was sick, so I need to (make up / turn in) the quiz.”
  4. What is the difference between drop a class and drop out?
Show suggested answers
  1. No, in most cases. A prerequisite must be finished first. If you think you have equal experience, ask your advisor.
  2. In the syllabus. Each instructor sets the rule, so it can be different in each course.
  3. make up You missed the quiz, so you need to do it later. Turn in means submit.
  4. Leaving one course, or leaving college. Drop a class means leaving one course. Drop out means leaving school completely.
